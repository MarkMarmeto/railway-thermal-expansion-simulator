## Who They Are

Maya is preparing a physics exhibit and needs to explain the relationship between temperature, material, expansion, and joint clearance without relying on a long lecture. She understands the basic idea of thermal expansion but needs the interface to connect the equation to a visible rail movement and an immediately readable result.

## End Goals

1. Change the three approved inputs and quickly understand what changed.
2. Demonstrate both an open joint and an insufficient-clearance result using credible numbers.
3. Explain the variables, formula, assumptions, and exaggerated visual movement to an audience.

## Experience Goals

- Feel confident that the animation and numbers agree.
- Stay focused on the physics instead of troubleshooting controls or hidden setup.
- Know when a result is a simplified teaching model rather than a claim about actual buckling.

## Behaviours & Context

- **Environment:** Standard classroom laptop and browser, possibly with an audience watching the screen.
- **Frequency:** Rehearses examples several times, then uses the app in a short live exhibit.
- **Proficiency:** Comfortable with sliders and dropdowns; benefits from visible units, labels, safe defaults, and plain-language explanations.

## Frustrations

- A result that changes without the rail scene or status changing with it.
- Scientific terms or units that are present but not explained.
- A closed-gap result that appears to show rail overlap or claims to predict buckling.

## Scenario

Maya opens the app before her demonstration, selects Steel, sets 55°C and a 6 mm initial gap, and sees 3.6 mm expansion, 2.4 mm remaining gap, and Open. She then uses the Aluminum example to show insufficient clearance, points to the formula and assumptions, and explains that the rail movement is exaggerated for visibility while the numerical result remains physically calculated.
