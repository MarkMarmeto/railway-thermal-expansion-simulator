## Who They Are

Elena evaluates the exhibit by checking whether the physics is correct, the assumptions are visible, and a student can explain the result without unsupported claims. She values a small working demonstration over a visually ambitious system that introduces unrelated engineering or thermodynamics.

## End Goals

1. Verify that displayed calculations match the specified linear-expansion and signed-clearance equations.
2. See that the three approved controls and required visual behaviors work reliably in a local browser.
3. Confirm that the exhibit communicates its limits, accessibility basics, and non-goals honestly.

## Experience Goals

- Be able to audit the example calculations quickly.
- Trust that the visual scene is driven by the same state as the numerical results.
- See clear evidence that scope has not expanded into fake force, buckling, fluid, or train simulation.

## Behaviours & Context

- **Environment:** Standard laptop browser, often reviewing while a student presents live.
- **Frequency:** Checks the app during implementation and performs a final exhibit review.
- **Proficiency:** Strong subject-matter and assessment judgment; needs concise technical explanations and reproducible examples rather than implementation detail.

## Frustrations

- Approximate material coefficients presented as certified engineering data.
- A visually impressive animation that cannot be reconciled with the formula or worked examples.
- Missing local run instructions, inaccessible controls, blank-page startup failures, or extra controls outside the approved scope.
